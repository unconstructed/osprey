## How the runner works

The rules are the reference rules (`dip_buy`, `constant_mix`, `scheduled`, ...): `nautilus.decider` takes each rule's
own decision function, and a Nautilus strategy calls it at every bar's close, with the position, entry and exit
state that Nautilus's own fills give. So the two runners make their decisions with one piece of code (the owner's
option B, 2026-11-25), and parity compares the accounting alone. Each bar goes in as two quotes, its open and its
close; an order decided at a close fills at the next bar's open (hard rule 4).

**`BacktestEngine`, not `BacktestNode`.** AGENTS.md rule 1 of "Running many simulations" names `BacktestNode`. The node
builds the same engine from a config and reads bars from the catalog itself; the engine takes the fee model and the
data directly. The results are the same, and the rule's point (one run per config, disposed after use) holds. Using
the node instead is a change of wiring, not of numbers; the owner can ask for it.

**Documented differences from the reference** (TESTING.md #61):
- A trade of at most `MIN_LEG` = 1e-9 of the wallet is rounding noise and is not sent; the reference trades it and
  pays gas. On real data this happened once (below).
- Nautilus keeps money to 12 decimals, so its numbers differ from the reference's in the last digits; a full buy is
  sized so cash never goes below zero.

## Throughput (one core, `scripts/nautilus_throughput.py`)

Synthetic bars of the real history's length; the engine's cost depends on bars and fills, not on where the prices
came from. Real-data timings above agree (about 0.03 s per daily run, 0.35-0.5 s per hourly run).

```
bars                                   rule                                                        fills  Nautilus /h/core  reference /h/core
daily, 3,420 bars (2017-2026)          dip_buy (drop 10%, 20 bars, stop 10%, hold 30)                169            59,052            728,289
daily, 3,420 bars (2017-2026)          50/50 rebalance every 30 bars                                 114            64,234            912,469
daily, 3,420 bars (2017-2026)          50/50 rebalance every bar (worst case: a fill on every bar)   3419            12,364            289,891
hourly, 52,560 bars (6 train years)    dip_buy (drop 10%, 20 bars, stop 10%, hold 30)                132             8,431             91,597
hourly, 52,560 bars (6 train years)    50/50 rebalance every 30 bars                                1752             6,333            101,054
```

Sizing: a 2,000-trial search on daily bars takes about 2 CPU-minutes on Nautilus; on hourly bars about 15
CPU-minutes. The reference is 10-25x faster. Results that count run on Nautilus (hard rule 3), and so must the luck
controls that re-run backtests for them, unless the owner decides otherwise (director report 2026-11-25, question 1).
