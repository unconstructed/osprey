## What was built

- `lab/runner/nautilus_portfolio.py`: `portfolio._simulate` on a NautilusTrader v2 `BacktestEngine`. One engine
  per run, two instruments (BTCX/USDX, ETHX/USDX) on one cash account, `DexFeeModel` fees. `run(rule, ...)` calls
  a rule with its module's `_simulate` swapped for the Nautilus one for the length of the call. So every rule built
  on `portfolio._simulate` runs unchanged, and so does its own post-processing: `portfolio.rebalance`,
  `random_rebalance`, `holdmix.hold_mix`, `trend.trend_backtest` (with and without the stop) and `trend.run_states`.
  The decision code is the rule's own (option B). `wrap(rule)` gives a picklable `BacktestFn`.
- `lab/costs/fees.py`: `RECUT_TAG`, a zero-commission order that carries out portfolio.py's pro-rata fee sharing for
  a coin that sits at its target while the other coin trades.
- `tests/test_nautilus_portfolio.py` (30 tests).
- `scripts/nautilus_portfolio_parity_real.py`: the real-data check below, with throughput.

## Documented differences (TESTING.md #65)

1. **Round-off legs** (at most `MIN_LEG`, 1e-9 of the book) are never trades. A fill whose legs are all round-off is
   not sent, whereas portfolio.py pays gas for it, as in #61. A round-off leg inside a real fill is re-cut at no cost,
   which is what portfolio.py's pro-rata fees do. A target of round-off size (for example 1e-126) is never bought.
2. **Extra orders that fill nothing.** When the whole book is in one coin and the band is 0, Nautilus keeps cash
   below one unit step. The rule can then see a weight of 0.999999999999 and order, but nothing fills. Only
   `attrs["orders"]` differs.
3. The rule sees weights to 12 decimals, and a leg below one 1e-12-unit step cannot be sent. Both are as in the
   single-asset runner.

Each difference is pinned by a hand-built test.
